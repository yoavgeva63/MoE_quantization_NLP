\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\todo{P1 --- the problem. A Mixture-of-Experts layer replaces a dense feed-forward block
with many experts and a small router that selects top-$k$ of them per token
\citep{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch}. Routing is a discrete
$\arg\text{top-}k$, so unlike a dense matrix multiply it can change its output
\emph{categorically} under a small perturbation. Post-training quantization perturbs
every weight. Motivate why that asymmetry matters.}

\todo{P2 --- the cheap structural fix. Routers are a vanishing fraction of the
parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params}), so excluding them from quantization costs almost
nothing in memory. State the hypothesis in one sentence.}

\todo{P3 --- the live disagreement, and our honest positioning. \citet{fu2025eaquant}
already run experts at W4A4 with the router at W8A8, so router protection is their
\emph{baseline}, not our invention --- say this plainly. Meanwhile
\citet{routequant2026} argue that a full-precision router is insufficient, because
quantization error in expert \emph{outputs} still shifts the next layer's router logits,
and therefore quantize the router and correct it with rank-aware losses. Nobody has
measured the size of either term under a controlled single-variable design with no
calibration. That measurement is what this paper contributes.}

\todo{P4 --- what we do. Three policies differing in exactly one respect, two
architectures, three bit-widths, no calibration data anywhere, bootstrap confidence
intervals over sequences, a parameter-matched placebo control, and an offline four-cell
decomposition of the residual drift.}

\todo{P5 --- findings, referring to Figure~\ref{fig:headline}. Router protection
produces large and replicable reductions in routing drift with disjoint confidence
intervals in all six model $\times$ bit-width cells; the placebo reproduces the
unprotected baseline, so the effect is router-specific; but a substantial residual
remains, and the attribution shows its dominant source \emph{changes} with precision.
Frame the answer as ``necessary but not sufficient''.}

\noindent
\todo{P6 --- contributions. Suggested four bullets: (i) the first placebo-controlled
measurement of router precision as an isolated variable in MoE PTQ, with bootstrap CIs,
on two architectures and three bit-widths; (ii) an adjudication of the
\citet{fu2025eaquant} / \citet{routequant2026} disagreement, answered quantitatively
rather than rhetorically; (iii) an offline four-cell attribution separating the router's
own rounded weights from upstream activation drift, requiring no additional GPU runs;
(iv) an open, gated implementation in which every run asserts what it actually
quantized.}

\subsection{Mixture-of-Experts routing}
\label{sec:bg-moe}

\todo{Define an MoE layer. For hidden state $x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, a router
$W_r \in \mathbb{R}^{E \times d}$ produces logits $z = W_r x$; the layer selects the
top-$k$ experts by $z$ and combines their outputs weighted by the renormalised softmax
over the selected entries only. Give the equation. Stress that the selection is discrete
and that $W_r$ is tiny relative to the $E$ expert matrices. Cite
\citet{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch,jiang2024mixtral}.}

\todo{Define the \emph{effective routing weights}: the post-top-$k$, renormalised,
zero-elsewhere vector that actually multiplies expert outputs. Explain why this differs
from the full softmax, which is dominated by the long tail of experts that are never
selected and can therefore move a great deal while the computation path is unchanged.
We report both for that reason.}

\subsection{Post-training quantization}
\label{sec:bg-ptq}

\todo{Define weight-only per-axis symmetric integer quantization: for each output
channel, $w \approx s \cdot q$ with $q$ an $N$-bit integer and $s$ a per-channel scale.
State that this is the only quantizer used here, applied identically at 8, 4 and 3 bits
via a single configuration class \citep{or2025torchao}, so that a trend across
bit-widths reflects precision alone and not a change of kernel or algorithm.
Contrast with calibration-based PTQ --- GPTQ \citep{frantar2023gptq}, AWQ
\citep{lin2024awq}, SmoothQuant \citep{xiao2023smoothquant}, LLM.int8()
\citep{dettmers2022llmint8} --- and say that our question is deliberately about a
\emph{calibration-free structural} safeguard, which is why none of those appear as
baselines.}

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:bg-metrics}

% Every metric used in the paper is defined here and nowhere else. Keep the definitions
% terse but complete; a reader must be able to reconstruct them from this subsection.
\todo{Define each metric in one or two sentences, in this order, and give the formula
for the first three.
\textbf{Routing KL}: $\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{gold}} \Vert p_{\text{cand}})$ over the full
expert softmax, averaged over tokens and layers.
\textbf{Top-1 flip rate}: the fraction of (token, layer) routing decisions whose
$\arg\max$ expert differs from gold. This is the headline metric because it counts
decisions that actually changed.
\textbf{Top-$k$ Jaccard distance}: $1 - |S_{\text{gold}} \cap S_{\text{cand}}| /
|S_{\text{gold}} \cup S_{\text{cand}}|$ over the selected expert sets; since both sets
have size $k$, the union is $2k$ minus the intersection.
\textbf{Expert-usage entropy}: entropy of the marginal expert load, normalised by
$\log E$; the collapse detector. Note that we also report per-token routing entropy,
because the marginal alone conflates load imbalance with router confidence.
\textbf{Jensen--Shannon divergence}: reported alongside KL because KL is unbounded and
explodes when the candidate places near-zero mass where gold had some.
\textbf{Output-distribution drift}: a truncated KL over the gold model's top-$M$ token
ids ($M = 64$) at every 16th scoring position, plus top-1 agreement --- included because
perplexity is blunt and a model can shift its output distribution noticeably while
perplexity barely moves.
\textbf{Perplexity}: WikiText-2, non-overlapping 1024-token windows.}

\todo{Define the bootstrap. All intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals with
200 resamples, and \emph{whole sequences are resampled, not individual tokens}. Explain
why in one sentence: tokens within a sequence are correlated, so token-level resampling
would produce dishonestly tight intervals. This matters because the entire Part~1 claim
rests on intervals being disjoint.}

\paragraph{The apparent disagreement, and why it is not one.}
\todo{\citet{routequant2026} argue that freezing the router is \emph{insufficient},

\subsection{The placebo control}
\label{sec:placebo}

\todo{This is the load-bearing control and deserves its own paragraph. Routers are
0.03\% of OLMoE and 0.02\% of Qwen (Table~\ref{tab:params}). If protecting \emph{any}
0.02\% of the weights bought the same improvement, the finding would be about
high-precision weights in general, not about routers. \placebo{} therefore quantizes all
routers, exactly as \uniform{} does, and instead protects a randomly chosen non-router
module whose parameter count matches the total router budget to within 25\%; the choice
is seeded for reproducibility. Report what it selected: on both models the budget is met
by a \emph{single} expert projection matrix, so \placebo{} protects one module where
\mixed{} protects 16 (OLMoE) or 24 (Qwen).
\note{This asymmetry is real and you must state it rather than let a reader discover it.
It is parameter-matched but not depth-matched. Carry the caveat into
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}; do not bury it.}}

On both models the selection is a single expert down-projection in a single mid-depth
layer: \texttt{model.layers.14.mlp.experts.10.\allowbreak down\_proj} on OLMoE
($1.000\times$ the router budget) and
\texttt{model.layers.19.mlp.experts.45.\allowbreak down\_proj} on Qwen
($0.978\times$), both re-derived offline from the recorded
\texttt{placebo\_seed}~$=0$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-verification}). Measured from
the \gold{} router logits, those modules are on the compute path for 11.4\% (OLMoE) and
5.0\% (Qwen) of routing tokens in one layer, against routers that every token reads in
every layer --- a token~$\times$~layer exposure gap of $140\times$ and $477\times$.

\subsection{The exposure-matched control}
\label{sec:attention-control}

% This subsection exists because the placebo, by construction, cannot address the
% competing explanation "any high-precision island on the every-token, every-layer main
% path helps". ATTENTION is the only available policy whose protected weights have the
% routers' exposure profile. Numbers below are from the correctness gates in
% results/*/summary.md and the module census in notes/method_audit.md.
\attn{} quantizes every router, exactly as \uniform{} does, and instead protects all
attention projections: 64 modules and 268{,}435{,}456 parameters (3.88\%) on OLMoE, 96
modules and 402{,}800{,}640 parameters (2.81\%) on Qwen, or roughly $128\times$ the
router parameter budget. Attention is read by every token in every layer, which is the
exposure profile the routers have and the \placebo{} does not. All 16 (resp.\ 24)
routers remain quantized under \attn{}, verified per router by name
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-gates}), so it is a genuine contrast with \mixed{} rather
than a second \mixed{} run.

\todo{One paragraph on why this is the control \placebo{} cannot be. The two controls
answer different questions: \placebo{} rules out ``any equally-sized set of weights in
BF16 helps'', \attn{} rules out ``any high-precision island on the every-token,
every-layer main path helps''. Matched \emph{exposure} rather than matched \emph{parameter

harder test on routing. Note that at \texttt{nn.Linear} granularity no policy can be

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\todo{Write each of the following as one or two sentences. Do not soften them.
\textbf{(1) The placebo is matched on parameter count and on nothing else.} On both models
the router budget is met by a single non-router projection matrix, so \placebo{} protects
one module in one layer while \mixed{} protects 16 or 24 modules spread across every
layer. The gap that matters is not depth but \emph{activation exposure}: measured from the
\gold{} router logits, the protected module is on the compute path for 11.4\% (OLMoE) and
5.0\% (Qwen) of routing tokens in one layer, against routers read by every token in every
layer --- a token~$\times$~layer exposure gap of $140\times$ and $477\times$. The placebo
therefore rules out ``any equally-sized set of weights in BF16 helps'' and cannot rule out
``any high-precision island on the every-token, every-layer main path helps''. It is also
$n = 1$: one module drawn once, at \texttt{placebo\_seed}~$=0$. At \texttt{nn.Linear}
granularity no control can be matched on count and exposure at once in either
architecture, since the smallest non-router linear layer is already $16\times$ the
per-layer router budget; closing that would require quantizing row slices of a module,
which the quantizer cannot express.
\textbf{(1b) What \attn{} does and does not settle.} It closes the exposure confound for
\emph{routing} but not for \emph{perplexity}, where it matches or beats \mixed{}
(Section~\ref{sec:divergence}): we can claim routers are the most efficient place to spend
precision per protected parameter, not the best place for end-task quality. \attn{} is also
not parameter-matched, so it separates exposure from role only if protecting \emph{more}
weight on the main path cannot help \emph{less}, and we have no policy matched on both
count and exposure to check that.
\textbf{(2) One corpus, one language, one task.} WikiText-2 perplexity and routing only;
no downstream task evaluation, so we cannot say whether the routing changes we measure
translate into task degradation.
\textbf{(3) Weight-only quantization.} No activation quantization, so our setting is not
directly comparable to W4A4 results such as \citet{fu2025eaquant}'s.
\textbf{(3b) Our perplexities are not on their scale.} \citet{fu2025eaquant} report FP16
WikiText-2 perplexity of 7.49 for OLMoE-1B-7B where our BF16 \gold{} run gives 8.36 on the
same checkpoint. We have not reconciled the gap, which is most likely evaluation protocol
(sequence length, stride, or split handling); until we do, we compare policies only within
our own runs and never against published absolute numbers.
\textbf{(4) No calibration-based baseline.} By design --- the question is about a
calibration-free structural choice --- but it means we cannot say how large the remaining
headroom is relative to GPTQ-style methods.
\textbf{(5) No memory or latency measurement.} torchao quantizes for real, but we run on
GPUs where the dequantized compute path dominates and we measure quality, not throughput.
Do not imply a speedup.
\textbf{(6) Saturation at INT3.} Qwen INT3 is unusable under both policies, so one sixth
of the sweep carries no evidential weight, in Part 1 or Part 2.
\textbf{(7) The attribution uses a strided subset} (every 8th token, 4{,}096 positions
per layer) and is reconstructed offline in a different numerical path from the run; the
reconstruction gate bounds but does not eliminate that gap. The same stride limits the
paired-difference bootstrap and the load-shape statistics in
Table~\ref{tab:collapse}, which is why the intervals we quote are the full-sample
marginal ones (Table~\ref{tab:gate}) and why starved-expert counts are taken from the
full-resolution fields.
\textbf{(8) Two architectures is not a survey.} Both have hidden size 2048 and both use
a clean \texttt{nn.Linear} router; we did not evaluate a DeepSeek-style
\texttt{nn.Parameter} gate, only defended against it.
\textbf{(9) Perplexity carries no interval anywhere.} Per-window negative log-likelihood
is not retained, so the first column of every results table is a point estimate over all
288{,}448 (OLMoE) / 298{,}785 (Qwen) scored tokens. Every inferential claim rests on the
routing metrics, which do carry sequence-level intervals --- and this is a real limit on
Section~\ref{sec:divergence}, where the \attn{}-versus-\mixed{} perplexity comparison is
therefore a comparison of two point estimates.}
